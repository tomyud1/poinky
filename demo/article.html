Quadcopter flight control

From Openpedia, the free encyclopedia
Quadcopter
Rotors4
ControlMotor speed only (no rudder, no flaps)
Typical usePhotography, mapping, inspection, racing
Flight controllerUpdates motor speeds 400–8,000 times per second

A quadcopter steers without any moving control surfaces. Everything it does (climbing, turning, tilting and moving) comes from changing the speed of its four motors.[1]

Basic principle

Each propeller pushes air down, producing thrust roughly proportional to the square of its speed. When the four thrusts add up to the drone's weight, it hovers.

Torque balance. A spinning propeller also twists the body the opposite way (reaction torque). Two rotors spin clockwise and two counter-clockwise, placed diagonally, so the twists cancel and the drone keeps its heading.[2]

Yaw (turning)

To turn, the flight controller speeds up one diagonal pair and slows the other by the same amount. Total thrust stays the same, so the drone doesn't climb or sink, but the twisting forces no longer cancel and the whole body rotates.[3]

Pitch and roll

To move forward, the rear rotors (3 and 4) spin faster than the front ones (1 and 2). The drone tilts nose-down, and part of its thrust now points backward, pushing it forward. Rolling left or right works the same way with the side pairs.

Hovering

Even when "still", the flight controller corrects hundreds of times per second using a gyroscope and accelerometer, because wind and small differences between motors constantly push it off balance.
